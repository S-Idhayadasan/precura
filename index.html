Most women can tell you exactly why they put it off.

A metal speculum. Full pelvic exposure on an exam table. That's what conventional cervical screening still asks of every woman, first-time patients most of all — and it's exactly the kind of appointment that gets rescheduled, then avoided, then forgotten. Even once a sample is collected, the wait for a lab result runs 24 to 72 hours. Every one of those delays is a chance for a preventable cancer to go undetected a little longer.

Roughly 200+ women die of cervical cancer in India each day — about one death every 7 to 8 minutes. Sources: Ministry of Health & Family Welfare, Lok Sabha reply (Jul 2025); PLOS ONE systematic review & meta-analysis (2022); CAPED India.

From sample to result, in three steps.

just a urine sample and a reader that does the rest.

01

Collect

The device captures any HPV shed naturally in a routine urine sample — no clinical procedure needed.

02

Process

The sample is processed with the reagents that is presented in the sample collection tube and then it is put into the device.

03

Detect

The device gives the result in 20 minutes whether the patient has HPV positve or negative.

Under 20 minutes
start to finish, compared with 24–72 hours for a conventional pap smear sent to a lab.
